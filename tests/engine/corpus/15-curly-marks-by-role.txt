# A curly mark already in the text is classed by its role in the active locale and variant, not by
# its shape (F1): in German, Czech and de-CH „ and ‚ open and “ and ‘ close; in the German book style
# » and › open and « and ‹ close. That holds in the same run and across an inline tag, where the mark
# is the scan’s own output from the run before. In fi and sv, where ” and ’ both open and close, a
# curly mark is told by what is on either side of it, as a straight one is (F2).
# Then: in depth mode a closer takes the level of the quotation it closes (FU4); inside a curly
# quotation an inch mark or a plural possessive is not its closer when the curly closer still
# follows (FU5, FU6); es guillemets-outer has a third level, the RAE order « “ ‘ (FU8); and cases
# that pin the scanner’s level bookkeeping (FU1).
# Expected output is written from the locale’s pairs, not copied from the engine.
